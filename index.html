The situation

Shared garage.
Borrowed fob.

You use a communal garage — an apartment block, a rented space — where you can't touch the motor or the gate controller. All you have is a key fob.

This gives you a hands-free way to open the door from your car's Android Auto screen. No internet. No cloud. No hub. The ESP32 presses the fob button for you.

Built right

HMAC-SHA256 Auth

Your password never travels over the air — only a hash of a fresh random nonce. Replay attacks are impossible by design.

Android Auto native

Built on the Car App Library. A single button on the car screen — safe to glance at from the driver's seat, try-again on failure.

Voice commands

Say "Open the garage" through the car mic or steering wheel button — Google routes the intent straight to the BLE command. All 8 languages.

Geofence auto-open

Set a radius on the map in Settings. When you enter the zone the gate opens automatically — no tap, no phone interaction. Works with or without Android Auto: if AA is connected it fires on the car screen; otherwise it uses speed and activity detection. A notification confirms every auto-fire.

Live presence indicator

A low-power background BLE scan shows a green dot when the opener is in range — on both the phone screen and the car display.

Renter-friendly option

Can't modify the fob? Option B uses a relay to switch the fob's battery power — copper tape discs in the battery slot, no soldering inside. Fully reversible when you move out.

Wear OS companion

Open the garage directly from your wrist — same animated button, haptic feedback on the result, and a watch tile for one-tap access from the watch face. No phone interaction needed.

Home Assistant integration

Wall-powered at home? Flash with WiFi credentials and the ESP32 joins your network, exposing a PIN-authenticated webhook. Fire it from any HA automation — BLE and the Android app keep working in parallel.

The flow

From phone to gate
in under a second

1
You tap, speak, or just drive up
One button on your Android Auto display, a voice command ("Open the garage" in 8 languages), or nothing at all — with geofence auto-open enabled the app fires automatically when you approach.
2
Phone sends an HMAC command over BLE
The app hashes a random nonce with your password and sends only the hash — your password never leaves the phone.
3
ESP32 verifies and fires
The ESP32 at your garage validates the HMAC, triggers the relay, and sends a status notification back.
4
Screen confirms — gate opens
The ring fills green on success, shows "Try Again" on failure so you never have to scramble while driving. Typical round-trip: under 1 second.
Pick your setup

Three trigger options.
One firmware.

The ESP32 firmware handles all three — pick the one that fits your situation. The relay module is recommended for most setups.

A
Recommended

Relay module

A small relay module shorts the fob's button pads when triggered. Beginner-friendly — the module's onboard driver handles everything, no transistor biasing needed. Use a 3V coil (e.g. Songle SRD-03VDC-SL-C) directly on the ESP32's 3.3V rail.

~$1–2 Soldering required
B
Renter-friendly

Relay + power switching

Same relay module as Option A, but instead of shorting the button pads, it switches the fob's battery ground rail. Copper tape discs slide into the battery slot — no soldering inside the fob. A small clip holds the button depressed. The fob fires every time it gets power. Fully reversible.

No fob modification ~$1–2
C

Transistor

A single NPN transistor shorts the fob's button pads. Lowest power draw (~20 mA vs ~70 mA for relay). Requires understanding transistor biasing and a 1 kΩ base resistor. Best if power budget is critical.

~$0.10 Advanced
Deployment

In the car or at
the garage entrance

In the car

Best for solo

ESP32 and fob both ride in the car, powered off the USB socket. BLE always works because everything is in the same vehicle. Zero range concerns.

  • ✓ Auto-fires when you pull up to the gate
  • ✓ Charges whenever the car is on
  • ✓ Nothing to install, nothing to maintain at the garage

At the garage

Multi-user

ESP32 lives near the gate, powered by wall outlet, power bank, or solar. Anyone you share the password with can open it from their own phone.

  • ✓ Supports multiple users and vehicles
  • ✓ Deep sleep between polls (~12 months on a power bank)
  • ~ BLE range 10–30 m through walls — plan placement

Your password never leaves the phone

Each connection generates a fresh random nonce on the ESP32. The phone hashes the nonce with your password using HMAC-SHA256 and sends only the hash. The ESP32 verifies it locally. A captured packet is worthless — the nonce is used once and discarded.
